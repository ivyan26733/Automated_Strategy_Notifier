The goal

What exactly are we building?

Before the journey — here's the destination, in plain words.

We are building an automated stock recommendation system for the Indian market (NSE).

The idea: When two moving averages on a stock's weekly chart cross in a bullish way — called a "Golden Cross" — it often signals the start of a big upward move. We hold the stock until the averages cross back down (the "Death Cross"), then exit.

The system should automatically:
  • Scan all 973 NSE stocks every week
  • Filter only the strongest signals (high volume, strong fundamentals)
  • Rank those signals using news, financials, and management tone
  • Alert us when a high-quality setup appears

Phase
4

Speed optimisation — 8× faster

The trend backtest was taking over 50 minutes to run. The bottleneck: for each Golden Cross per stock, we were scanning all data again to find the Death Cross. For a stock like RELIANCE with 10 Golden Crosses, that was 10 separate full-data scans.

Fix: find all Golden and Death Crosses in one single pass per stock, then use binary search to pair them. Runtime dropped from 51 minutes to 6.5 minutes.

✓ 8× speed improvement

Fix

Critical price bug — found & fixed

A Claude Opus review found that our entry price and exit price were on different scales. Entry used the raw stock price; exit used a price that was retroactively scaled down by all dividends paid since 2016. This created a fake "loss" at the start of every trade.

In 2016, this fake loss was ~7%. In 2022, ~2%. Fixed by putting both prices on the same adjusted basis. All backtests were re-run from scratch.

Backtest strategy

How the backtest actually works

Why we chose this approach, what the rules are, and how the code runs it — in plain words and a visual flow.

Why GC → DC (not fixed windows)?

Our early backtest tested 5, 10, 20, 60-day holds after a Golden Cross. But that's not how we trade — we hold until the trend reverses (Death Cross), which could be 1 month or 3 years depending on the stock.

Cutting a trade at day 60 when the stock is still running is leaving money on the table. The Death Cross is the natural exit signal — it tells us momentum has flipped. This also matches exactly what the website shows in the Returns tab, so we can compare like-for-like.

Why weekly charts, not daily?

Daily EMA9/EMA20 crossovers are noisy — they fire every few weeks and create too many false signals. Weekly bars smooth out short-term volatility. A Golden Cross on the weekly chart means the medium-term trend has genuinely shifted up, not just a 3-day bounce.

This is also what most serious technical analysts use for swing and positional trades, which is the style we're targeting — hold weeks to months, not hours to days.

Discoveries

Things we found out along the way

Some of these changed how we think about the strategy completely.

💡
The 90% win rate on the website is misleading — not wrong
The website shows a 90% win rate for 10 years. Our backtest shows 35%. Both numbers are correct — they measure different things.

The website shows 90% because: (1) Open trades from 2020 are still showing today's high prices — those multi-baggers look like wins. (2) Only stocks still alive today are shown — stocks that crashed and died are invisible. (3) India was in a strong bull market 2020–2024.

Our 35% is the real, clean number: all trades closed at Death Cross, including the ones that lost money, including dead stocks. This is the honest metric.
📊
35% win rate is actually good — here's why
35% sounds like you lose 65% of the time. But look at what happens when you do win vs when you lose:

Profit Factor = 4.38 — for every ₹1 the strategy loses, it makes ₹4.38.

This is the signature of trend-following: many small losses, few massive winners. Think of CGPOWER going from ₹9.75 to ₹650 (6,625% return), or CG Power, TIPS Music, Xpro India. One trade like that covers 10 losses. The math works.
📦
Volume filtering works for trend trades, not short-term holds
We tested volume filtering on two different strategies:

Short-term (5–60 days after GC): Volume filtering is WEAK. High-volume signals don't outperform low-volume ones over a few weeks.

Trend strategy (GC → DC): Volume filtering is STABLE. All 5 thresholds beat the baseline across all 11 lookback variations we tested. Best pick: 20-25 day lookback, 1.0–1.5× volume threshold — keeps 50%+ of signals and adds ~1% win rate improvement.
⚠️
Future data can't be used to score past signals
We planned to score Golden Cross signals using stock fundamentals from Screener.in. Problem: Screener.in shows today's financials. If we score a signal from 2018 with 2026 ROE data, we're cheating — we're using information that didn't exist in 2018.

The right approach: for each signal, use only the quarterly financial report that was published before that signal date. This is harder to collect but it's the only honest way to backtest a ranking system.
What went wrong

Our honest failures

Things that wasted time, misled us, or had to be re-done.

🐛
Price data bug — 6+ hours wasted
Entry and exit prices were on different scales due to how yfinance adjusts historical data. The fixed-window results were garbage for months. Every conclusion about short-term performance was wrong. Caught by an independent Opus review, not us.
⏱️
Backtest took 50 minutes — wrong algorithm
The first version of the trend backtest called a full data scan for every single Golden Cross signal per stock. For 973 stocks with multiple crosses each, this was extremely slow. Had to redesign the algorithm from scratch.
📉
Tested the wrong strategy (fixed windows)
Phase 2 tested 5/10/20/60-day holds after a Golden Cross. But we don't trade that way — we hold until the Death Cross. Three phases of work on fixed-window analysis that doesn't match our real trading plan.
🏭
Pre-listed stocks slipping into backtest
Some stocks in our universe had data starting near their IPO date, causing near-zero entry prices and fake multi-thousand-percent returns. We added an audit step to exclude stocks with less than 3 months of data before the signal date.

The solution is a hard stop-loss to cap downside. Next experiment: add a −10% stop to MB and see if it unlocks the win-rate advantage cleanly.

Key learning: 2018/19 is a stock-universe problem, not a market regime problem

Nifty 50 was fine in 2018/19. The IL&FS credit crisis destroyed mid-cap finance, infra, and NBFC stocks while large caps held up. A large-cap index filter cannot detect this. The real solutions are: (a) a Midcap-specific or breadth-based regime filter, (b) a fundamental quality screen rejecting high-leverage / illiquid companies, or (c) sector exclusion rules that pause NBFC/infra entries during credit stress cycles.

Study Roadmap

What you need to study to crack the negatives

The data tells us exactly what is broken. Each problem maps to a specific area of study. Work through these in order — each one directly fixes a known weakness in the current system.

URGENT
Stop-Loss & Position Sizing

The problem it solves: 15.1% of MB trades lose >20%. One catastrophic trade can wipe out 3–4 winners. Without a stop-loss, the profit factor improvement from better entry is neutralised by blow-up trades.

ATR (Average True Range)
Volatility-based stop: set stop at 2×ATR below entry so low-volatility stocks have tighter stops than high-volatility ones
Fixed % stop vs trailing stop
Fixed −10%: simple, easy to backtest. Trailing: follows price up, locks in gains. Study both and compare
Kelly Criterion
Optimal bet sizing given win rate and win/loss ratio. At 39.7% WR and 6.7× W:L, Kelly says bet ~30% of capital. Full Kelly is too aggressive — half-Kelly is safer
Time stop
If trade shows no momentum after 4–8 weeks, exit regardless of price. Stagnant positions tie up capital that could be in a new signal
HIGH PRIORITY
Market Regime Detection

The problem it solves: 2018 win rate = 14%, 2019 = 11%. Both were broad market downturns (IL&FS crisis, trade war). In a bear market, even good GC signals fail because the entire market is falling. We took 1,724 bad trades in just those two years.

Nifty 500 macro filter
Only take signals when Nifty 500 is above its 200-week EMA. If the index is in a downtrend, pause all new entries. Simple rule, dramatic improvement in bear years
India VIX as a gate
When India VIX >25, volatility is extreme and trend signals fail more often. Study: does pausing entries at VIX >20 improve the 2018/2019 win rate?
Breadth indicator
% of NSE 500 stocks above their 200-day MA. If <40%, the market is sick — don't buy breakouts. This filters regimes before individual stock signals
Advance-Decline ratio
Ratio of advancing stocks to declining stocks over 20 days. A falling A/D line in a rising market = bearish divergence. Classic regime signal
HIGH PRIORITY
Entry Timing: Retracement vs Breakout

The problem it solves: MB enters at the breakout (highest price). Buying the breakout means buying when retail FOMO is highest. A retracement entry (waiting for a small pullback after the breakout) gives a better price and lower risk. The median return being negative in both strategies suggests we're often entering at local highs.

Fibonacci retracements
After a GC, wait for 38.2% or 50% pullback before entering. Classic technique: "buy the dip in a confirmed uptrend." Reduces entry price and improves reward:risk
RSI pullback entry
Enter when RSI dips to 40–50 zone after a GC (brief cooling off) rather than chasing RSI >70. Lower entry price, less FOMO risk
Bollinger Band squeeze
A GC signal combined with a Bollinger Band squeeze (low volatility contracting before breakout) is much stronger than a GC alone. Signals coiled spring ready to release
MACD histogram
MACD histogram turning positive while EMA cross happens = double confirmation. Study: does requiring MACD confirmation reduce the 60% loss rate without killing too many winners?
MEDIUM
Quantitative Metrics to Measure Strategy Quality

The problem it solves: We currently compare strategies with win rate, avg return, and profit factor. But these don't capture drawdown risk or account for how long money was in the market. Two strategies with the same profit factor can have very different real-world risk profiles.

Sharpe Ratio
Return / risk. Above 1.0 = good, above 2.0 = excellent. Forces you to account for volatility, not just return. Study how to compute it for a portfolio backtest
Sortino Ratio
Like Sharpe but only penalises downside volatility. Better for trend strategies where upside volatility (big wins) is welcome. Use Sortino alongside Sharpe
Max Drawdown & Recovery
Worst peak-to-trough loss in the portfolio. How long did it take to recover? A strategy with 40% max drawdown needs a 67% recovery — psychologically brutal
Monte Carlo simulation
Randomly shuffle the order of trades 10,000 times. What is the range of outcomes? If shuffled results vary wildly, the strategy is fragile. If stable, it's robust
MEDIUM
Fundamental Quality as a Filter (not just a score)

The problem it solves: Our catastrophic losers (−97.8% worst trade) are likely frauds, heavily leveraged companies, or stocks with deteriorating fundamentals. Fundamental filters should eliminate these before any technical signal is trusted.

Quality investing framework
Study Sanjay Bakshi (FinSight) and Buffett's approach adapted to India. ROCE >15%, debt-free or low debt, consistent cash flow > profit. These are hard floors, not scores
Piotroski F-Score
9-point fundamental health score (profitability, leverage, operating efficiency). F-score ≥7 = quality. Study: do GC signals from high F-score stocks have dramatically better outcomes?
Altman Z-Score
Predicts bankruptcy risk using 5 financial ratios. Z < 1.8 = distress zone. Eliminate these stocks entirely. This alone might cut the −97.8% worst trades out
Screener.in / BSE XBRL
Learn to pull structured quarterly data: Screener.in API or BSE XBRL filing. This is the data layer for everything above. Understand the filing date vs quarter-end distinction
LATER
Portfolio Theory & Correlation

The problem it solves: Right now, 10 concurrent signals might all be from Banking sector. A sector-specific event (RBI rate decision, NPA cycle) could blow up all 10 at once. Portfolio construction needs to account for correlation between positions.

Sector exposure limits
Simple rule: max 2–3 positions per sector in the portfolio at any time. Forces diversification without needing complex math
Correlation matrix
Study Pearson correlation of weekly returns across sectors. If two holdings are 0.8+ correlated, they behave like one position — no benefit to holding both
Modern Portfolio Theory basics
Markowitz efficient frontier: understand how combining uncorrelated assets reduces portfolio volatility without reducing return. Foundation for any multi-stock system

Honest gaps between where we are and where the system needs to be.

What's next

The roadmap — in order

These steps build on each other. We can't jump to step 3 without step 1.

1

Build a portfolio simulator

Write Python code that takes a list of ranked signals, picks the top N (e.g., 10), runs them as concurrent trades with GC→DC exits, and outputs monthly P&L, CAGR, and maximum drawdown. This gives us a real scoreboard to measure whether any ranking system helps.

2

Collect historical fundamentals with filing dates

Scrape BSE quarterly filing dates for all 973 stocks. For each historical Golden Cross signal, find the last quarterly result available before that date. Store ROE, ROCE, revenue growth, D/E ratio, and promoter holding in Supabase — all tagged with the date they were known, not today.

3

Score signals by fundamentals — run portfolio sim

Build a fundamental scorer that ranks each signal 0–40 points based on how the stock compares to its sector peers (percentile ranking). Run the portfolio sim: top-10 by score vs random-10 vs no filter. If the score improves CAGR or Sharpe, it's real and we use it.

4

Add news veto layer (red flags only)

Google News RSS for each stock. If the headline contains fraud / SEBI probe / insolvency / default — that signal is eliminated, no score given. This is not scored (we can't backtest 2016 news) but it protects against obvious disasters in live trading.

5

Add concall quality layer

Download BSE quarterly concall PDFs, use Claude API to extract structured signals (management guidance raised/cut, order wins, tone). Score 0–30 points. For live signals only — not backtested. Combine with fundamental score for a final conviction rating.

6

Automate the pipeline + alerts

GitHub Actions runs the scanner weekly. New Golden Crosses are scored automatically. A Telegram/email alert fires for signals scoring above threshold (e.g., 65/100). Also starts a Decision Log — every recommendation frozen with its score components so we can measure accuracy over time and improve the system with real forward data.

The bottom line

Two backtested strategies, 973 stocks, 2016–2022. GC→DC: profit factor 4.38, 35.3% win rate. Momentum Breakout: profit factor 4.42, 39.7% win rate — but 57% more catastrophic losers. The edge is real; the risk management is the missing piece. The next three experiments are: (1) ATR stop-loss on MB, (2) market regime filter on both strategies, (3) Piotroski quality screen to eliminate the worst-trade stocks. Each one directly attacks a known number. That's the work ahead.
