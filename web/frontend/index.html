The strategy scans NSE equities on weekly OHLCV data for EMA9 × EMA20 crossovers. A Golden Cross (GC) — EMA9 crosses above EMA20 — generates a long entry signal; a Death Cross (DC) — EMA9 crosses below EMA20 — is the exit. As of September 2026, a GC fires only when all six criteria below are simultaneously met. The first three criteria have always been enforced; the last three (ATR spread, EMA20 slope gate, volume) were added after backtesting showed they improve signal quality. Fundamentals are used for ranking only — they never decide whether a signal fires.

Signal Selection — 6 Hard Gates (ALL must be satisfied)
1
EMA9 crosses above EMA20
prev_EMA9 ≤ prev_EMA20 AND curr_EMA9 > curr_EMA20
The core golden cross signal. EMA9 must have been at or below EMA20 last week and now crossed above it this week. Both EMAs computed on weekly closing prices using Wilder's exponential smoothing (alpha = 2/(period+1)).
Since V1
2
Fresh cross only — no re-firing while above
above_state = False when signal fires · resets when EMA9 ≤ EMA20
An above state variable prevents duplicate signals while EMA9 stays above EMA20. A new signal can only fire after the state has fully reset (EMA9 dropped back below EMA20 in a subsequent week).
Since V1
3
Weekly candle (developing bar included)
signal date = observation_date of the latest weekly bar, including the in-progress week
The scanner evaluates the crossover on the current weekly bar even while that week is still open, so a cross is surfaced on the day it happens rather than after Friday's close. Trade-off: these signals can repaint — a cross detected mid-week may fall back below EMA20 before the week closes, in which case it was never a confirmed weekly golden cross. Such a stock leaves the Active EMA tab once EMA9 drops back under EMA20.
Changed Sep 2026
4
EMA20 monotonically rising for 4 consecutive weeks
EMA20[i] > EMA20[i−1] > EMA20[i−2] > EMA20[i−3] > EMA20[i−4]
The medium-term trend must be actively turning up, not just flat. Strictly monotonic: every one of the 4 prior weekly EMA20 values must be lower than the next. A net increase (EMA20[i] > EMA20[i−4]) without monotonicity does not pass — it catches the beginning of trend inflections, not sustained rises that may then reverse.
NEW — Sep 2026
5
EMA9–EMA20 spread ≥ 0.05 × ATR(14) on weekly data
(EMA9 − EMA20) ≥ 0.05 × ATR(14, weekly OHLC, Wilder's smoothing)
Filters out vanishingly thin noise crosses where EMA9 barely nudges above EMA20 with no real price separation. The threshold is ATR-scaled — not a fixed percentage — so it automatically adjusts for a stock's own volatility regime. ATR is computed using Wilder's recursive formula seeded on the first 14 true ranges. If ATR data is not yet available (fewer than 15 weekly bars), this gate is skipped.
NEW — Sep 2026
6
Daily volume on crossover day > 20-day average daily volume
vol(obs_date) > mean(vol, 20 prior trading days) → vol_ratio_20d > 1.0
Volume on the last trading day of the crossover week must exceed the average of the preceding 20 trading days. If daily data is absent for a stock, this gate is skipped (not treated as a rejection). Counterintuitive finding: within tech-filtered signals, low-volume crosses (vol < 20d avg) significantly outperformed high-volume ones in 2016–2022 — see Volume Analysis section below.
NEW — Sep 2026
Signal Labels & Outcome Metrics
return_pct — primary metric
(DC exit price − GC entry price) / GC entry price × 100
Total GC→DC return. Primary outcome for 2016–2022 because all signals in that window are fully resolved (DC has occurred and the trade is closed). Win = ≥ 10%, Loss = < 0%, Neutral = 0–10%.
ret_13w — secondary metric
Return from GC entry price to 13 weeks later (fixed horizon)
Unbiased across all years including 2023–2026 open signals. Used in Phase 1 factor analysis to study feature importance without survivorship bias from variable hold durations. Win definition: ≥ 10% in 13 weeks.
Win / Loss Label
Win = return_pct ≥ 10% · Loss = return_pct < 0%
Of 7,555 closed signals, 584 are neutral (0–10%) and are excluded from win-rate calculations. The model is evaluated on its ability to separate clear winners from clear losers. Baseline win rate among W/L signals: 30.0%.
Expanding-Window Scoring
Quartile boundaries = computed from all prior signals only · burn-in = 500
All feature scores use expanding-window quartile breakpoints. A signal in 2018 is scored using boundaries computed from 2016–2017 signals only — zero lookahead bias. The first 500 signals are excluded from scoring during the burn-in period.
Phase 1 — Technical Factor Analysis

Four technical features measured at the moment of the Golden Cross were studied for predictive power on both ret_13w and return_pct. Each feature is converted to a 0–3 point score using expanding-window quartile boundaries (burn-in: 500 signals). The four scores are summed into a Composite Score (0–12) that acts as the primary technical quality filter. A higher composite score means all four technical conditions simultaneously indicate a high-quality entry.

price_52w_position
(Close − 52-week Low) / (52-week High − 52-week Low)
Where the stock sits within its annual range at the time of the GC. A value near 1.0 means the GC is happening at or near new 52-week highs — a strong, upward-trending structure, not a random dead-cat bounce. A GC occurring while the stock is in the bottom half of its range is structurally weaker.
Top quartile (> 0.67): +6.9 pp lift on ret_13w
ema_diff_pct
(EMA9 − EMA20) / EMA20 × 100
Percentage spread between EMA9 and EMA20 at the moment of the cross. A very tight spread (near 0%) is a fresh, just-occurred crossover. A very wide spread (> 5%) means the crossover already happened earlier and much of the trend move may already be in. Optimal entry: 0.1% – 2% spread.
Optimal range 0.1%–2%: +4.2 pp lift on ret_13w
momentum_4w
4-week price return at GC entry date
Short-term price momentum immediately before the cross. Captures whether the stock was already accelerating into the crossover (bullish confirmation) vs. a weak bounce in a broader downtrend. Positive 4-week momentum at GC entry confirms the cross is part of a building trend.
Top-quartile 4w momentum: +3.8 pp lift on ret_13w
ema20_slope
Rate of change of EMA20 over the prior 4 weeks
Slope of the longer-term EMA — is the medium-term trend itself turning up, flat, or still declining? A rising EMA20 at the moment of the GC means the underlying trend is confirming the crossover. A flat or falling EMA20 is a warning that the cross may be a false signal.
Positive slope: +4.1 pp lift on ret_13w

6-Criteria Conjunction — Backtest Impact vs Previous Strategies

The conjunction (AND of all 6 hard gates) is not equivalent to the composite score filter used in previous backtests. The composite score awards partial credit — a signal can score 10/12 by being excellent on three features and mediocre on the fourth. The conjunction requires all conditions simultaneously. Since the ATR-spread criterion is not pre-computed in the historical dataset, the table below uses proxies: ema20_slope > 0 ≈ criterion 4, ema_diff_pct > 0.5% ≈ criterion 5, vol_ratio_20d ≥ 1.0 = criterion 6 exactly.

How to read the proxy: The proxy conjunction is an approximation — ema20_slope>0 is weaker than strict monotonic 4-week rising, and ema_diff_pct>0.5% is a flat percentage threshold vs the true ATR-scaled gate. Actual live signal counts will differ from the proxy. The proxy understates strictness, so actual 6-criteria signals will be fewer than 1,680.

Key insight: The 6-criteria conjunction reduces signals to ~745 (proxy) vs 1,174 for composite ≥10, but win rate drops from 37.3% to 34.9%. The conjunction and the composite score are not equivalent filters. The composite score with its partial-credit model is a better predictor at the population level than a strict AND conjunction. This does not mean the criteria are wrong — the new gates filter signal generation for quality reasons beyond predictive win rate alone (e.g. filtering thin noise crosses via ATR gate; ensuring trend is genuinely turning via monotonic EMA20 rise). The composite score is complementary: use the 6 criteria to select signals, then use the composite score and fundamental rank to prioritize among those that pass.

The technical composite score decides which signals to take; the fundamental score decides which among those to prioritize.

Volume Factor Analysis — Counterintuitive Finding

Signal criterion 6 requires the crossover day's daily volume to exceed the 20-day average — the intuition being that high volume confirms smart-money participation. The backtest tells a different story. Using the exact criterion measure (vol_ratio_20d ≥ 1.0 — daily vol vs its 20-day average on the GC week), low-volume signals (vol_ratio_20d < 1.0) consistently outperform high-volume signals within every tech-filtered subset.

⚠
Counterintuitive Finding: Low Volume Outperforms High Volume
Within tech-filtered signals (composite ≥ 10), the win rate for low-volume crosses is 41.1% (+11.1 pp lift) vs 35.3% (+5.3 pp) for high-volume crosses — a 5.8 pp gap favoring LOW volume. This pattern holds in 5 of 7 individual years, with the largest gap in 2017 (44.2% low vol vs 27.4% high vol). Criterion 6 as currently specified (vol > 20d avg) filters in the weaker signals and filters out the stronger ones. This criterion is implemented as specified; the data is shown here so the team can decide whether to revise it.

Return Distribution & Expectancy per Trade

GC→DC returns are highly right-skewed: the median is negative (most trades lose money or barely break even, because many GC signals are false starts that exit quickly at a small loss), while the mean is strongly positive because winning trades can return 100–500%+ while losers are typically capped at −15 to −25% (the death cross cuts the position before catastrophic loss). The practical metric is expectancy = P(win) × avg_winner_return + P(loss) × avg_loser_return.

Key Conclusions
Best Strategy Found
+12.2 pp
Tech composite ≥ 10 + Fundamental top-half (score ≥ 44) achieves 42.2% win rate vs 30.0% baseline. Generates ~70 actionable signals per year. Average return per closed trade: +54.6%. This is the recommended live-trading configuration.
Fundamentals Alone = Weak Signal
+0.9 pp
Applying only the fundamental hard-rejection filter (removing 34% of stocks) gives just +0.9 pp lift. Balance sheet quality alone does not predict short-to-medium term EMA crossover trade outcomes. The technical setup is the primary predictor.
Fund Score is Monotonically Predictive
Q2→Q4
Within tech-filtered signals (≥ 9 or ≥ 10), win rate rises monotonically with fundamental score: 33.4% (Q2) → 37.2% (Q3) → 39.4% (Q4). The gradient is consistent at both tech thresholds. Fundamental score is a genuine, additive signal when used inside a technical filter.
Top-Half Beats Top-Quartile
2× signals
Tech ≥ 10 + Fund top-half (42.2%, n=505) beats Tech ≥ 10 + Fund top-Q (40.1%, n=247) in both win rate AND signal count. Over-restricting fundamental quality gives up signal volume without proportionate win-rate gain. Use score ≥ 44 (top-half), not ≥ 60.
Bear Markets Show Largest Alpha
+16.6 pp in 2022
In 2022 (FII selloff, rate hikes), baseline win rate was 22.1% but the combined strategy reached 38.7% — the largest single-year lift. In 2019 (IL&FS aftermath), combined hit 13.3% vs 6.0% baseline. Fundamental quality provides the most protection when markets are broadly falling.
Structural GC Losers — Blacklist Needed
Known Gap
Some stocks with high fundamental scores (e.g. CASTROLIND: score=68, ROCE=63%) are structural GC losers due to their long-term price pattern generating repeated false crossovers. A stock blacklist mechanism is designed but not yet implemented in the live scanner.
Volume Gate Inverts Expected Result
−5.8 pp
Criterion 6 (vol > 20d avg) was hypothesized to improve signal quality by confirming momentum. In practice, within tech-filtered (composite ≥ 10) signals, the high-volume subset achieves 35.3% WR vs 41.1% for the low-volume subset — a 5.8 pp penalty from enforcing the volume gate. The pattern holds in 5 of 7 years. The criterion is implemented as specified and the decision to keep, revise, or remove it rests with the team. If reversed (require vol < 20d avg), the best configuration becomes Tech ≥ 10 + low vol at 41.1% (+11.1 pp).
6-Criteria Signal — Live Since Sep 2026
3 new gates
The scanner now enforces all 6 signal selection criteria. The three new gates added in Sep 2026 are: (4) EMA20 monotonically rising for 4 consecutive weeks; (5) EMA9–EMA20 spread ≥ 0.05 × ATR(14) — ATR-scaled to the stock's own volatility; (6) daily volume on crossover day > 20-day average. These run as hard gates in signal generation — not scoring features. The composite score and fundamental rank are still applied for prioritization after a signal fires.
Data Gaps & Known Limitations
  • ✓
    Signal Criteria 2–4 — EMA20 Slope Gate, ATR Spread Gate, Volume Gate — Now Implemented Previously only 3 of 6 signal criteria were enforced. As of September 2026, the scanner implements all six: (4) EMA20 monotonically rising for 4 weeks — strict monotonic check at generation time; (5) EMA9–EMA20 spread ≥ 0.05 × ATR(14) computed with Wilder's smoothing on weekly OHLC data; (6) daily volume on obs_date > 20-day average daily volume, with graceful skip if daily data is absent. These are hard gates in signal generation, not post-hoc filters.
  • ⚠
    Promoter Pledge % — Hard Rejection Rule NOT Enforced The strategy spec requires rejecting stocks where promoter pledge > 5% of promoter shares pledged to lenders. This data is loaded via AJAX on Screener.in and cannot be extracted from static HTML without a headless browser or a paid data API. The pledge rule is disabled. ~750 stocks have promoter holding % captured correctly; pledge % is stored as NaN (not 0% — the field is absent, not zero).
  • ⚠
    Bank-Specific Metrics — Neutral Defaults Used in BankScorer BankScorer requires GNPA (Gross Non-Performing Asset %), NIM (Net Interest Margin %), CASA ratio, and CET1 capital ratio. None of these are available from yfinance. The 26 bank stocks are scored, but approximately 25–30% of their scorecard (sub-sections covering asset quality and funding stability) uses neutral/midpoint default values. Bank scores may be systematically understated or overstated.
  • ⚠
    Fundamental Data is a Point-in-Time Snapshot Applied to Historical Signals Current fundamental scores are computed from the most recent available financial data (~FY2025/FY2026). These same scores are applied retroactively to GC signals from 2016–2022. This introduces a mild look-ahead bias — a company's 2026 balance sheet quality is used to score a 2018 GC signal. For strategy validation this is acceptable, but production use requires time-aligned fundamental data (e.g., using the FY data available at the time of each signal).
  • ⚠
    5-Year Revenue / PAT CAGR Often Missing yfinance provides approximately 4 years of annual financial statement data for most NSE stocks. The scorecard uses 3-year Revenue CAGR and PAT CAGR (well-covered: ~99% of stocks). Any metric requiring a 5-year lookback will frequently be NaN and fall back to neutral scoring, potentially understating long-term earnings consistency.
  • ⚠
    Stock Blacklist Not Implemented Some stocks are structural GC losers — their price characteristics generate repeated false golden cross signals regardless of fundamental quality. No automated detection or blacklist mechanism exists yet. These stocks will continue to appear in filtered signals until the blacklist is implemented. Known example: CASTROLIND (fundamental score = 68, ROCE = 63%, but consistently produces losing GC trades due to low-volatility, range-bound price action).
  • ⚠
    Nifty Market Regime Filter Not Implemented The strategy currently takes signals regardless of broader market regime. In 2018 and 2019, baseline win rates were 7.2% and 6.0% respectively — taking signals in those environments is deeply unprofitable. A Nifty-above/below-200-week-EMA regime filter (or equivalent breadth filter) would suppress signals in confirmed bear markets and improve risk-adjusted returns substantially. This is the highest-priority pending enhancement.

EMA & ATR Formulas
Exponential Moving Average (EMA)
alpha = 2 / (period + 1) EMA[0] = Close[0] ← seed (first available bar) EMA[i] = alpha × Close[i] + (1 − alpha) × EMA[i−1] EMA9: period = 9, alpha = 2/10 = 0.2000 EMA20: period = 20, alpha = 2/21 ≈ 0.0952
Computed on weekly closing prices. Runs forward from the first available bar — no look-ahead bias.
True Range (TR) — per weekly bar
TR[i] = max( High[i] − Low[i], ← intrabar range |High[i] − Close[i−1]|, ← gap up from prior close |Low[i] − Close[i−1]| ← gap down from prior close ) TR[0] = undefined (no prior close for first bar)
ATR(14) — Wilder's Smoothing on Weekly OHLC
Step 1 — Seed (bars 1–14): ATR[14] = mean(TR[1], TR[2], …, TR[14]) ← simple average Step 2 — Recursive (bar i > 14): ATR[i] = (ATR[i−1] × 13 + TR[i]) / 14 General form for period n: ATR[i] = (ATR[i−1] × (n−1) + TR[i]) / n
Wilder's method uses (n−1) as multiplier, not a standard exponential alpha. This gives the ATR a longer memory than a conventional EMA of the same period. Period = 14 weekly bars here.
EMA Difference (Spread)
ema_difference = EMA9 − EMA20 ema_difference_pct = (EMA9 − EMA20) / EMA20 × 100 Positive → EMA9 above EMA20 (uptrend / active GC) Negative → EMA9 below EMA20 (downtrend / after DC)
Composite Score (0 – 12)
Four technical features scored 0–3 each via expanding-window quartiles. Sum = composite score 0–12. Used for ranking only — never determines whether a signal fires.

Expanding-window quartiles — no look-ahead: Quartile boundaries are recomputed from all signals seen before the current one. A 500-signal burn-in period applies — the first 500 signals all receive score 0 while the distribution accumulates. This means live scoring is never contaminated by future data.

Outcome & Backtest Metrics
return_pct — Primary Metric
return_pct = (exit_price / entry_price − 1) × 100 entry_price = weekly close on Golden Cross signal date exit_price = weekly close on Death Cross date (EMA9 crosses below EMA20) Open signals → exit_price = latest weekly close (mark-to-market)
Win threshold: return_pct ≥ +10%. Loss threshold: return_pct ≤ 0%. Neutral zone (0–10%) excluded from the W/L denominator in win-rate calculations.
ret_13w — Fixed-Horizon Secondary Metric
ret_13w = (price[entry + 13 weeks] / price[entry] − 1) × 100 Fixed 13-week window from entry date — no exit trigger. Position is valued at week 13 whether EMA cross still holds or not.
Unbiased metric: unlike return_pct, ret_13w is not affected by how long a trend lasts. Each signal gets an identical 13-week evaluation window.
Win Rate
win_rate = winners / (winners + losers) × 100 winners = signals where return_pct ≥ +10% losers = signals where return_pct ≤ 0% neutral = 0% < return_pct < 10% → excluded from denominator Baseline (2016–2022): 2,091 / 6,971 = 30.0%
vol_ratio_20d — Volume Gate Input (Criterion 6)
vol_ratio_20d = daily_volume(obs_date) / mean(daily_volume, prev 20 trading days) "prev 20 trading days" = 20 sessions strictly before obs_date (excludes obs_date itself) Data source: daily (not weekly) volume series
