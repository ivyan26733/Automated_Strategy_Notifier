Does buying golden crosses actually make money?

We took the exact six rules the scanner uses today and replayed them on the weekly charts of every NSE stock from January 2016 to September 2026. Every time a stock passed all six rules we "bought", and we "sold" when the trend turned down. 941 trades finished. Here is what happened.

Chapter 1

The idea: two lines racing on a chart

A stock's price jumps around every week. A moving average smooths that noise into a line that shows the direction. This strategy uses two of them, calculated from weekly closing prices:

  • EMA9, the fast line — follows roughly the last 2 months. Think of a sprinter who reacts quickly.
  • EMA20, the slow line — follows roughly the last 5 months. Think of a marathon runner who changes pace slowly.

When the sprinter overtakes the marathon runner, recent prices are rising faster than the longer trend. That moment is a golden cross and it is our buy. When the sprinter drops back behind, that is a death cross and it is our sell. Nothing else triggers a sale: no target price, no stop-loss.

Why "exponential"?

An EMA gives recent weeks more weight than older weeks, so it turns sooner than a simple average. Each week: new EMA = old EMA + α × (this week's close − old EMA), where α is 0.20 for EMA9 and about 0.095 for EMA20.

Prices are weekly closes from the scanner's local data, adjusted for splits and bonuses, so they may differ slightly from prices quoted at the time.

When does the scanner check?

Every weekday evening, using the current week so far, so a cross shows up the day it happens instead of waiting for Friday. The trade-off: a cross seen on Wednesday can disappear if the price falls back by Friday's close. This guide's backtest uses only finished weeks.

Chapter 4

The risks: read this before trusting any backtest

🌧️
Bad years come in pairs

In 2018–2019 only 12% of 106 signals made money. In 2024–2025 it was 23% of 199. You need the patience and the money to keep taking signals through a year or two of mostly losing trades.

🎯
A few trades make the money

The best 5% of trades produced 55% of all the gains. Skip signals because they "look bad" and you may skip the one that pays for the whole year.

📉
It has been getting weaker

Trades that made money: 46% before 2016, 39% in 2016–2022 and 35% since 2023. Money won per ₹1 lost fell from about ₹5.9 to ₹4.5 to ₹3.3. Still profitable, but the edge is shrinking.

🏁
Most trades lag the market

Compared with the Nifty Midcap index over the same weeks, only 30% of trades did better (trades since 2010). The average trade still beat the index by 13 points, again thanks to the big winners.

🧺
You need room for many positions

Since 2016 about 54 trades were open at the same time on average, and up to 124 at the peak. Following every signal with equal money needs a large account, or a rule for choosing among signals.

What this backtest cannot see
  • Companies that no longer exist. Only stocks listed today were tested. Firms that were delisted or collapsed are missing, which flatters the results.
  • Mid-week signals. The test uses finished weeks; the live scanner also reacts to the unfinished week, so live signals can come earlier, or appear and then vanish.
  • Real prices and costs. Trades are filled at the exact weekly close with no brokerage, tax or slippage in the headline numbers.
  • The future. Price data ends 4 Sep 2026. Past results describe how the rules behaved, not a promise of returns.
Chapter 5

Which rules help: three versions compared

Same stocks, same years (2016 to Sep 2026), same buy-and-sell mechanics. Only the checklist changes.

Every crossoverthe old rule Rules 1–4 & 6no volume rule Today's 6 ruleswhat the scanner runs
The trend rules do the heavy lifting

Requiring a fresh cross, a rising slow line and a real gap cuts signals by nearly 90% and lifts the share of winning trades from 32% to 38%. Winners get much bigger (typical +34% → +46%).

The volume rule halves signals without helping

Adding the volume check cuts signals from about 201 to 96 a year, but the win rate stays level (37.9% → 37.4%) and the average trade slips (+31.5% → +27.5%). It filters out as many good trades as bad ones.

Stricter rules mean bigger swings both ways

The filtered trades are stronger-trending stocks, so the typical loser is a little worse (−10% → −13%) while the typical winner is much better. The trade-off pays off.

What about volume in the earlier research?

A separate 2016–2022 study (win = +10% or more) found no clear volume effect overall: crosses on above-average volume won 31.0% of the time, quieter ones 29.1%. Among the highest-scoring crosses, quieter volume did better (41.1% vs 35.3%), but not in every year, and from 2023 on the gap nearly disappeared (41.9% vs 40.6%). Taken together: there is no reliable evidence that the volume rule improves results in either direction. Details are in the Deep dive.

Two different "win rates" on this page

Chapters 1–5 count any profit as a win and test the rules the scanner actually runs today. The Deep dive is earlier research on 973 stocks that counts only gains of +10% or more as wins and leaves out the 0–10% trades, so its numbers look lower (a 30% starting point). It found a stronger way to rank signals (technical score ≥ 10 plus good fundamentals: 42% vs 30%), but the scanner does not yet filter on it, so treat those figures as research, not as the live strategy's results.

Chapter 6

Five things to remember, then check yourself

  1. It follows trends. Buy when the fast weekly average crosses above the slow one, sell when it crosses back. No predictions, no targets.
  2. Expect to be wrong most of the time. About 63 in 100 trades lose, usually by 10–15%, and are closed within about three months.
  3. The profit comes from rare, long winners. The typical winner made about +49% and was held for about a year. Missing them is the biggest risk.
  4. Results swing hard by year. 2018–19 and 2024–25 were poor, and the edge has been shrinking.
  5. Backtests flatter reality. Missing delisted stocks, perfect prices and no costs. Treat these numbers as a best case, not a promise.

Phase 1 — Technical Factor AnalysisFour chart measurements scored 0–12. Crosses scoring 10+ won 37% of the time vs 30% for all crosses.

Four technical features measured at the moment of the Golden Cross were studied for predictive power on both ret_13w and return_pct. Each feature is converted to a 0–3 point score using expanding-window quartile boundaries (burn-in: 500 signals). The four scores are summed into a Composite Score (0–12) that acts as the primary technical quality filter. A higher composite score means all four technical conditions simultaneously indicate a high-quality entry.

price_52w_position
(Close − 52-week Low) / (52-week High − 52-week Low)
Where the stock sits within its annual range at the time of the GC. A value near 1.0 means the GC is happening at or near new 52-week highs — a strong, upward-trending structure, not a random dead-cat bounce. A GC occurring while the stock is in the bottom half of its range is structurally weaker.
Top quartile (> 0.67): +6.9 pp lift on ret_13w
ema_diff_pct
(EMA9 − EMA20) / EMA20 × 100
Percentage spread between EMA9 and EMA20 at the moment of the cross. A very tight spread (near 0%) is a fresh, just-occurred crossover. A very wide spread (> 5%) means the crossover already happened earlier and much of the trend move may already be in. Optimal entry: 0.1% – 2% spread.
Optimal range 0.1%–2%: +4.2 pp lift on ret_13w
momentum_4w
4-week price return at GC entry date
Short-term price momentum immediately before the cross. Captures whether the stock was already accelerating into the crossover (bullish confirmation) vs. a weak bounce in a broader downtrend. Positive 4-week momentum at GC entry confirms the cross is part of a building trend.
Top-quartile 4w momentum: +3.8 pp lift on ret_13w
ema20_slope
Rate of change of EMA20 over the prior 4 weeks
Slope of the longer-term EMA — is the medium-term trend itself turning up, flat, or still declining? A rising EMA20 at the moment of the GC means the underlying trend is confirming the crossover. A flat or falling EMA20 is a warning that the cross may be a false signal.
Positive slope: +4.1 pp lift on ret_13w

Volume Factor Analysis — Mixed EvidenceVolume made little difference overall; a quiet-volume edge among top-scored crosses was uneven and faded after 2022.

Signal criterion 6 requires the crossover day's daily volume to exceed the 20-day average — the intuition being that high volume confirms real buying interest. The evidence is mixed. Using the exact criterion measure (vol_ratio_20d ≥ 1.0 — daily vol vs its 20-day average on the GC week), volume barely matters across all signals (31.0% vs 29.1%). Within tech-filtered subsets, low-volume signals (vol_ratio_20d < 1.0) did better in 2016–2022, but the edge was uneven by year and shrank to about 1 pp after 2022.

⚠
Mixed Evidence: A Quiet-Volume Edge That Did Not Last
Within tech-filtered signals (composite ≥ 10), the win rate for low-volume crosses is 41.1% (+11.1 pp lift) vs 35.3% (+5.3 pp) for high-volume crosses — a 5.8 pp gap favoring LOW volume. Low volume led in 5 of 7 years at composite ≥ 10 (2016 and 2019 went the other way). But across all 6,971 signals volume made almost no difference (high 31.0% vs low 29.1%); from 2023 onward the composite ≥ 10 gap shrank to about 1 pp (high 40.6% vs low 41.9%, n = 313 / 172); and in the live-rule backtest, removing the volume rule left the win rate level while doubling the number of signals. There is no reliable evidence that the volume gate helps or hurts; its main effect is fewer signals.

Return Distribution & Expectancy per TradeMost trades lose a little and a few win big; filters make winners bigger rather than losers rarer.

GC→DC returns are highly right-skewed: the median is negative (most trades lose money or barely break even, because many GC signals are false starts that exit quickly at a small loss), while the mean is strongly positive because winning trades can return 100–500%+ while losers are typically capped at −15 to −25% (the death cross cuts the position before catastrophic loss). The practical metric is expectancy = P(win) × avg_winner_return + P(loss) × avg_loser_return.

Q2→Q4
Within tech-filtered signals (≥ 9 or ≥ 10), win rate rises monotonically with fundamental score: 33.4% (Q2) → 37.2% (Q3) → 39.4% (Q4). The gradient is consistent at both tech thresholds. Fundamental score is a genuine, additive signal when used inside a technical filter.
Top-Half Beats Top-Quartile
2× signals
Tech ≥ 10 + Fund top-half (42.2%, n=505) beats Tech ≥ 10 + Fund top-Q (40.1%, n=247) in both win rate AND signal count. Over-restricting fundamental quality gives up signal volume without proportionate win-rate gain. Use score ≥ 44 (top-half), not ≥ 60.
Bear Markets Show Largest Alpha
+16.6 pp in 2022
In 2022 (FII selloff, rate hikes), baseline win rate was 22.1% but the combined strategy reached 38.7% — the largest single-year lift. In 2019 (IL&FS aftermath), combined hit 13.3% vs 6.0% baseline. Fundamental quality provides the most protection when markets are broadly falling.
Structural GC Losers — Blacklist Needed
Known Gap
Some stocks with high fundamental scores (e.g. CASTROLIND: score=68, ROCE=63%) are structural GC losers due to their long-term price pattern generating repeated false crossovers. A stock blacklist mechanism is designed but not yet implemented in the live scanner.
Volume Gate: Mixed Evidence
½ the signals
The volume rule was expected to confirm momentum. Across all signals it made almost no difference (31.0% vs 29.1%). Within composite ≥ 10, high-volume crosses trailed low-volume ones in 2016–2022 (35.3% vs 41.1%), but the gap shrank to about 1 pp in 2023–2026, and in the live-rule backtest dropping the rule kept the win rate level (37.9% vs 37.4% since 2016) while doubling the number of signals. Its main effect is fewer signals, not better ones.
6-Criteria Signal — Live Since Sep 2026
3 new gates
The scanner now enforces all 6 signal selection criteria. The three new gates added in Sep 2026 are: (4) EMA20 monotonically rising for 4 consecutive weeks; (5) EMA9–EMA20 spread ≥ 0.05 × ATR(14) — ATR-scaled to the stock's own volatility; (6) daily volume on crossover day > 20-day average. These run as hard gates in signal generation — not scoring features. The composite score and fundamental rank are still applied for prioritization after a signal fires.

A–Z

Glossary: every term in plain words

ATR (Average True Range)
How far a stock's price typically moves in a week, averaged over 14 weeks. A volatile stock has a large ATR.
Backtest
Replaying a set of trading rules on past prices to see how they would have done. It shows how rules behaved, not what will happen next.
Closed vs open trade
A closed trade has been sold (a death cross happened). An open trade is still held; its result is not final.
Death cross
The week EMA9 falls back below EMA20. This strategy's only sell signal.
EMA (exponential moving average)
An average of past prices that gives recent weeks more weight, so it turns sooner than a plain average. EMA9 uses about 9 weeks, EMA20 about 20.
Expectancy
The average result per trade once winners and losers are combined: win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss.
Golden cross
The week EMA9 moves above EMA20. The starting point of a buy signal, if all six rules pass.
Median (typical)
The middle value when results are sorted: half did better, half did worse. Unlike the average, a few huge trades cannot pull it up.
Average (mean)
All results added up and divided by the number of trades. Big winners pull it far above the median here.
Percentage points (pp)
The plain difference between two percentages. 30% to 37% is +7 pp (not +7%).
Profit factor
Total money won by winning trades ÷ total money lost by losing trades, with equal money in each trade. Above 1 means profitable.
Repaint
A signal seen during an unfinished week that disappears by the week's close because the price moved back.
Signal
A golden cross that passed all six rules, so the scanner lists it.
Survivorship bias
Testing only companies that still exist today. Firms that failed are left out, which makes past results look better than they were.
Volume (20-day average)
The number of shares traded in a day, compared with the average of the 20 trading days before it.
Win rate
The share of finished trades counted as wins. In Chapters 1–5 a win is any profit; in the Deep dive it is a gain of 10% or more; on the Returns tab it is a stock whose ₹100 ends above ₹100.

The tipping points

      ATR is held at this week's ₹50.17. In reality it also moves a little with the week's high and low. The rules are listed in the order the code checks them; once one fails, the later ones are not reached.

      One thing to know before reading on

      All strategy maths uses weekly candles. The live scanner runs every weekday evening and includes the unfinished current week, so a signal can appear mid-week and, if the price falls back by Friday, disappear again. The backtests on Research & Backtests use finished weeks only.

      Prices & candles

      Weekly candle

      💬A candle summarises a whole week of trading in five numbers. The strategy looks at weeks, not days, to ignore day-to-day noise.

      Week = Monday to Friday Open = opening price of the week's first trading day High = highest price reached on any day that week Low = lowest price reached on any day that week Close = closing price of the week's last trading day (so far) Volume = total shares traded that week
      🧮 Worked example · M&M, 15–19 Jun 2020

      Open ₹505.15 (15 Jun) · High ₹520.95 · Low ₹492.00 · Close ₹500.05 (19 Jun). The close is the number every EMA below uses.

      📍 Where you see itSignal PriceCMPinput to every EMA

      The unfinished (developing) week

      💬The scanner does not wait for Friday. Each weekday evening it builds the current week's candle from the days so far, so its "close" is that day's close.

      On Wednesday: Open = Monday's open, High/Low = Mon–Wed range, Close = Wednesday's close, Volume = Mon–Wed total

      Watch out: a cross seen on Wednesday is re-checked every evening. If the price slips back before Friday's close the cross can vanish ("repaint").

      What the site leaves out

      💬Some stocks produce misleading crosses, so they are filtered before any number is shown. This silently changes every count and average.

      Hidden from every tab: • stocks on the database's circuit list (price locked at its limit) • stocks listed on NSE less than 90 days ago Returns tab only: • golden crosses within 182 days of a stock's listing are ignored • a death cross at the very start of the chosen period is skipped (the buy it closes happened before the period began)

      Why: a new listing's moving averages start from its first price, so its early "crosses" are noise, and a price stuck at a circuit limit does not trade normally.

      Moving averages

      Exponential moving average (EMA9 and EMA20)

      💬An average of weekly closes that leans towards recent weeks. Each week it moves a fixed share (α) of the way from its old value towards the new close. EMA9 moves 20% of the way, so it is quick; EMA20 moves about 9.5%, so it is slow.

      α = 2 ÷ (period + 1) EMA9: α = 2 ÷ 10 = 0.20 EMA20: α = 2 ÷ 21 ≈ 0.0952 EMA this week = EMA last week + α × (close this week − EMA last week) (the same as α × close + (1 − α) × EMA last week) First week of a stock's history: EMA = that week's close A week with no price: EMA carries over unchanged
      🧮 Worked example · M&M, week ending 19 Jun 2020 · close ₹500.05
      1. EMA9 = 433.8564 + 0.20 × (500.05 − 433.8564) = 433.8564 + 13.2387 = ₹447.10
      2. EMA20 = 437.8541 + 0.0952 × (500.05 − 437.8541) = 437.8541 + 5.9234 = ₹443.78

      Watch out: starting from the first close matches TradingView's ta.ema. Sites that start from a simple average will show slightly different values for a stock's first months, then converge.

      📍 Where you see itEMA9EMA20Research replay chart

      Why EMA9 turns first: how much each past week counts

      💬Unpacking the formula shows every past week still counts, but less and less. Weight of a week that was k weeks ago = α × (1 − α)k.

      EMA gap and EMA Diff %

      💬How far the fast line is above (positive) or below (negative) the slow line, in rupees and as a percentage of the slow line.

      EMA gap = EMA9 − EMA20 EMA Diff % = (EMA9 − EMA20) ÷ EMA20 × 100
      🧮 Worked example · M&M, 19 Jun 2020
      1. Gap = 447.0951 − 443.7775 = ₹3.32
      2. Diff % = 3.3176 ÷ 443.7775 × 100 = +0.75%

      Reading it: just after a cross the gap is small. A large Diff % usually means the cross happened weeks ago and much of the move has already happened.

      Signal rules: exactly what the scanner checks

      The code checks the rules in this order and stops at the first failure:

      1. At least 6 weekly candles
      2. Fast line crosses above slow line
      3. It is a fresh cross
      4. Slow line rose 4 weeks running
      5. Gap ≥ 5% of ATR
      6. Volume above 20-day average
      7. Signal

      Rules 1 & 2 · Golden cross, and it must be fresh

      💬The fast line has to move from at-or-below the slow line last week to above it this week.

      last week: EMA9 ≤ EMA20 this week: EMA9 > EMA20 and no cross already counted since EMA9 last fell to or below EMA20
      🧮 Worked example · M&M

      Week ending 12 Jun: 433.86 ≤ 437.85 ✓ · week ending 19 Jun: 447.10 > 443.78 ✓

      Watch out, a cross can be "used up": if a cross fails any later rule, the scanner still marks that move as seen. It will not signal a week later even if every rule passes then; EMA9 has to fall back to or below EMA20 and cross again.

      Rule 3 · The slow line rose 4 weeks in a row

      💬The bigger trend must already be turning up. Each of the last four weekly steps in EMA20 has to be a rise; one dip fails the rule even if the total went up.

      EMA20 this week > 1 week ago > 2 weeks ago > 3 weeks ago > 4 weeks ago
      🧮 Worked example · M&M, weeks ending 22 May → 19 Jun 2020

      423.5424 → 424.7622 → 430.4229 → 437.8541 → 443.7775 ✓ every step higher

      True range and ATR(14)

      💬ATR is the size of a typical weekly move. True range counts gaps too: if a stock opens far above last week's close, that jump is part of the move.

      True range (TR) = the largest of High − Low | High − last week's close | | Low − last week's close | ATR(14): first value = simple average of the first 14 weekly TRs after that = (last week's ATR × 13 + this week's TR) ÷ 14 ← Wilder's smoothing
      🧮 Worked example · M&M
      Week endingHigh − Low|High − prev close||Low − prev close|True range
      5 Jun 2020 (gap up from ₹436.35)51.1056.255.1556.25
      19 Jun 2020 (prev close ₹508.45)28.9512.5016.4528.95
      1. ATR on 19 Jun = (51.8046 × 13 + 28.95) ÷ 14 = (673.4598 + 28.95) ÷ 14 = ₹50.17

      Rule 4 · The gap is big enough

      💬A hairline cross often flips straight back. The gap must be at least 5% of the stock's typical weekly move, so a volatile stock needs a wider gap than a calm one.

      EMA9 − EMA20 ≥ 0.05 × ATR(14) (skipped, not failed, when ATR is not available yet: fewer than 15 weekly candles)
      🧮 Worked example · M&M, 19 Jun 2020

      Gap 3.3176 ≥ 0.05 × 50.1721 = 2.5086 ✓

      Rule 5 · Volume above its 20-day average

      💬Shares traded on the candle's last day must beat the average of the 20 trading days before it. It was added to confirm real buying interest.

      volume on the candle's last trading day > average volume of the 20 trading days before it (skipped, not failed, when fewer than 21 trading days of data exist)

        6-month breakout (the second strategy)

        Consolidation breakout

        💬Flags a stock whose price climbs above the highest weekly close it made between 6 and 2 months ago. Ignoring the last 2 months means the stock must have spent at least 2 months below that level before breaking out.

        reference high = highest weekly close among finished weeks dated between 6 months ago and 2 months ago signal = this week's close (so far) > reference high Breakout % = (close ÷ reference high − 1) × 100
        🧮 Illustrative example
        1. Highest weekly close between 6 and 2 months ago: ₹250.00
        2. Today's close: ₹262.00 > ₹250.00 → signal
        3. Breakout % = (262 ÷ 250 − 1) × 100 = +4.8%

        Watch out: there are no other filters and no exit rule. Its Return % is always CMP ÷ signal price − 1, and it can be recorded again on later days while the price stays above the reference.

        Median (typical) vs average

        💬The median is the middle trade once they are sorted. The average adds everything up, so a single huge winner can pull it far above what a typical trade looks like.

        median = middle value of the sorted returns (mean of the two middle values if the count is even) average = sum of returns ÷ number of trades
        🧮 Worked example · five trades: −12, −9, −6, +18, +160
        1. Median = the 3rd value = −6%
        2. Average = (−12 − 9 − 6 + 18 + 160) ÷ 5 = 151 ÷ 5 = +30.2%

        Average win, average loss, profit factor

        average win = mean of trades above 0% average loss = mean of trades at or below 0% payoff ratio = average win ÷ |average loss| profit factor = sum of winning returns ÷ |sum of losing returns| (same money in every trade)
        🧮 Worked examples
        1. Five trades: profit factor = (18 + 160) ÷ (12 + 9 + 6) = 178 ÷ 27 = 6.59×
        2. Today's rules since 2016: payoff = 97.4 ÷ 14.4 = 6.8× · profit factor 4.05×

        Expectancy and break-even win rate

        💬Expectancy is the average result per trade once winners and losers are combined; above zero means the strategy makes money over many trades. Break-even is the lowest win rate that still avoids a loss.

        expectancy = win rate × average win − (1 − win rate) × |average loss| break-even win rate = |average loss| ÷ (average win + |average loss|)
        🧮 Worked example · today's rules since 2016
        1. Expectancy = 0.374 × 97.4 − 0.626 × 14.4 = 36.43 − 9.01 = +27.4% per trade
        2. Break-even = 14.4 ÷ (97.4 + 14.4) = 12.9%, far below the actual 37.4%

        Percentiles and concentration

        10th percentile = the return that 10% of trades did worse than 90th percentile = the return that 10% of trades did better than top-5% share = sum of the best 5% of trades ÷ sum of all winning returns
        🧮 Today's rules since 2016

        10th percentile −21.7% · 90th percentile +106.2% · best 5% of trades = 55% of all gains
